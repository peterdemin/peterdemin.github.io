# Sail wind tunnel

A buildless, dependency-free WebGL experiment in calculated sail loading, fabric relaxation, and finite-span airflow. The mast is fixed into the ground and the boom is fixed in position. Only the fabric moves. This lives separately from the existing `sail.html` and `work/sail-explainer.html` examples.

## Run

From the repository root:

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8765 --bind 127.0.0.1
```

Open <http://127.0.0.1:8765/sail-wind-tunnel/>. The included generated classic-script `bundle.js` is designed to support directly opening `index.html` from disk by avoiding browsers' file-origin ES-module restrictions. Modular sources remain in `physics.mjs` and `main.mjs`; after editing them, regenerate the bundle with `node sail-wind-tunnel/build.mjs`. No CDN, package install, or hosting change is needed. Drag or use arrow keys to orbit; wheel or +/− to zoom. Pause preserves cloth and smoke while camera controls remain available. Wind controls still recompute a frozen-shape solution while paused. Reset preserves wind settings but clears cloth state and any numerical failure. Startup errors show an actionable message and HTTP preview link instead of an unexplained dark canvas.

The reported blank Safari file-mode page used the earlier ES-module entry point, which browsers restrict at file origins. The classic bundle removes that dependency and exactly matches the modular source. The shipped bundle is verified running over HTTP with visible geometry/smoke and functional controls. Direct-file Safari execution remains unverified: Safari automation encountered a macOS capture failure, and the in-app browser's security policy rejects file URLs. A temporary HTTP fixture with a deliberately missing bundle verified the visible startup error and recovery link. The preview server was restarted after the app restart stopped the previous process; it must remain running for HTTP links to work.

## Calculated model

- Coordinates: x follows the boom, y is vertical, z is transverse. Wind is `U [cos θ, 0, sin θ]`. The sail is 4.4 m tall, with 3.2 m foot and 0.25 m head chords, at 0.6 m above ground. The small head chord avoids a degenerate top row.
- **Flow:** a 6 × 10 horseshoe vortex lattice on the current fabric shape. Bound segments lie at quarter chord; no-normal-flow collocation points lie at three-quarter chord. A pivoted dense linear solve determines 60 circulation strengths. The same regularized finite-segment Biot–Savart field is used by boundary diagnostics and smoke, with a 0.035 m core and 70 m straight downstream wake. Incoming flow is horizontal; induced flow has all three components.
- **Kutta closure:** the conventional horseshoe lattice with downstream wake legs provides an implicit trailing-edge/Kutta closure. This is not an independently resolved or tested trailing-edge pressure condition. Wake legs retain their horseshoe circulation. Their strengths and positions change with each quasi-steady solve; wake roll-up and shed time history are omitted. For reverse wind, chord ordering reverses and the free leech becomes the aerodynamic leading edge. Reverse-flow cloth behavior is not validated.
- **Forces:** sum `ρ Γ (U + Vwake) × dl` over bound segments, using wake-leg induced velocity at each bound midpoint. Density is 1.225 kg/m³. An explicitly empirical drag force `½ρU² A Cf`, with `Cf = 0.008`, is added along the wind. Displayed drag is the projection of the summed model force along incoming flow. Its calculated induced and empirical skin contributions appear separately. Lift is the complete remaining transverse vector, including vertical force; the number is its magnitude. The signed horizontal component and vertical component appear in diagnostics. Neither number claims measured real-world total force. Total-force arrows have equal scale, 0.018 m/N, and render over the sail for visibility; optional small local arrows have their own 0.03 m/N scale.
- **Cloth and built-in fullness:** 77 vertices. The manufactured reference is a flat cut with `cutChord = nominalChord × (1 + 0.0339 × sin²(π spanFraction))`, and position `(cutChord × chordFraction, 0.6 + 4.4 × spanFraction, 0)`. The cut contains 3.39% excess width at mid-height relative to the nominal rig chord, tapering to zero at the foot and head. Structural edges and one shear diagonal per quadrilateral form two hinged triangles; all rest lengths are distances in this flat reference. The initial state matches them with zero strain. Luff and foot are straight and exactly pinned; leech and head remain free, so the nominal upper rig chord is a comparison envelope rather than another positional constraint. The cut metric stores excess cloth without imposing camber or its sign. Calculated forces alone fill it on either side. Its rest coordinates are never imposed on free nodes during dynamics.
- **Reversal correction:** the previous double-curved reference encoded intrinsic shell-like curvature, and adding both shear diagonals made every nonplanar quad a rigid tetrahedron. Aerodynamic forces reversed correctly, but this geometry created an artificial inversion barrier: part of the bag remained on its starting side. A developable flat cut and a single-diagonal triangular membrane remove these locks. There is no sign-based position change, color-only correction, reset on wind change, rest-length switch, or camber prescription.
- **Near-inextensibility:** structural and shear links use 10 MN/m numerical stiffness, weak two-hop bending proxies 0.5 N/m. This is a nearly inextensible distance-constraint approximation, not a calibrated woven material. XPBD uses compliance `1/k`, a 1/240 s step, and 80 Gauss–Seidel sweeps, with multipliers reset each substep. Small stretch remains from finite compliance and incomplete constraint convergence, and is measured in the UI. Loads distribute equally to a panel's four corners, including pinned corners. Aerodynamics refreshes each four cloth steps (60 Hz of simulated time). Shape feeds back to the flow solve.
- **Weight:** each vertex has mass equal to 0.22 kg/m² times its tributary reference area, assigning one quarter of each incident panel's reference area. This rest mass stays constant when the sail deforms; inverse mass is used consistently in both acceleration and XPBD corrections. Gravity is `[0, −9.81, 0]` m/s². Pinned vertices have zero inverse mass. Weight affects sag and loaded equilibrium; inertia and strong numerical damping (100/s) determine relaxation. Damping is a numerical aid, not a prediction of aerodynamic dissipation or physical settling time.

The revised flat-cut cloth weighs about 1.698 kg. The no-wind gravity check lowers its mass-center by only about 0.124 µm versus an unchanged weightless control. The strong distance constraints carry weight mostly in-plane, and a perfectly flat start has no transverse perturbation. The model does not resolve spontaneous gravitational buckling or realistic free draping; this check verifies mass/gravity accounting rather than claiming visible no-wind sag.
- **Coupling:** flow assumes the current shape is stationary. The calculation deliberately omits cloth-motion boundary velocity and fluid added mass. Strongly damped relaxation finds approximate loaded shapes; its time history is not a validated transient aeroelastic simulation. Early stress testing found explicit moving-surface coupling unstable, so it is not shipped.
- **Smoke:** 35 deterministic tracers at seven heights (0.9, 1.8, 2.8, 3.8, 4.8, 5.2, 5.6 m), five transverse offsets per height. Each is integrated with the midpoint/RK2 method through `velocityAt`, with accumulated simulation time at approximately 60 Hz. Lines show particle histories/pathlines through the evolving field, not simultaneous streamlines or continuously emitted streaklines. Long trails reset to their upstream seeds. Above-head paths are purple. There are no prescribed curved paths or random drift.
- **Model domain and failures:** Only forward directions within ±20°, or zero wind at any angle, produce displayed results. Outside that domain, forces, load colors/vectors, smoke, and cloth updates are withheld. Gray cloth holds its last shape; returning within range resumes without reset. Unsupported physics is distinct from numerical failure and not a physical prediction of zero force. Separately, singular or excessive-circulation solutions stop numerically; a step above 8% structural/shear strain is rolled back and requests reset. An injected extreme supported-domain load tests that stop. Forces are not silently clamped.

## Broadside discontinuity

At ±90° the raw algorithm changes aerodynamic leading-edge ordering, moving bound/control locations. A frozen manufactured cut at 6 m/s gave a largest foot-panel force of 1238.59 N at 89.9° versus 123.29 N at 90.1°. The worst pre-switch panel saw about 39.9 m/s wake velocity and about 1225 N transverse wake-induced load. At 0.03 m/N the local arrow was roughly 37 m long. Tiny residuals and finite condition numbers did not validate broadside attached-flow physics. Unsupported predictions are now withheld, not smoothed or clipped. Colors follow a stable material normal (+z when flat), independently of aerodynamic ordering. Both faces share a color.

## Limits that matter when interpreting the scene

This is inviscid, attached, incompressible potential flow over an infinitely thin lifting surface, coupled to a coarse fabric model. Use modest forward incidence, roughly ±20°, for qualitative comparisons. Nominal global incidence is not a guarantee that every deformed panel stays in an attached-flow regime. The direction control includes larger and reversed angles, but their interactive results are withheld. Raw diagnostic calls may explicitly use `solveAerodynamics({allowUnsupported: true})`; interactive calls never do.

The simulation does **not** resolve viscous separation, stall, turbulence, leading-edge vortices, wake roll-up, mast/boom flow obstruction, ground effects, sail self-contact, or collisions with the rig. It has no calibrated woven-fabric constitutive model. Bending links are geometric proxies. Finite constraint sweeps leave small numerical extension, particularly at maximum wind; its measured strain is not a trustworthy real-material strain prediction. Skin friction is an empirical estimate, not a viscous fluid solution. Control-point impermeability does not guarantee impermeability everywhere between panels; tracers can pass through the coarse surface away from collocation points. The core smooths vortex singularities and limits visible detail. Smoke is zero-volume passive tracer material and does not affect flow.

Finite-span horseshoes do produce calculated vertical and transverse velocity around and above the head. This supports examining **finite-span head/tip flow**, but does not establish real separated “top spill.” Real sail effectiveness, stall onset, and viscous spilling cannot be inferred accurately from this demo. Low algebraic residual, symmetry, and refinement consistency establish internal numerical checks only; they are not experimental validation. The browser caps catch-up work, so simulated time may run slower than wall time on slow devices.

Method references: [NASA, Remarks on vortex-lattice methods](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730046944); [Macklin, Müller & Chentanez, XPBD](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/XPBD.pdf).
