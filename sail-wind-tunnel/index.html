Physics & limits

60 horseshoe vortices enforce no normal flow at panel control points on each frozen shape. Downstream wake legs impose an implicit Kutta condition. A finite 70 m straight wake and 3.5 cm vortex core define the velocity field. Smoke trails are passive particle histories integrated through this field with midpoint integration.

Panel forces use Kutta–Joukowski with wake-induced velocity; drag combines calculated induced drag with an empirical skin-friction estimate (Cf = 0.008). It is not a measured total drag. These forces drive a 77-node XPBD fabric of 0.22 kg/m², with gravity and strong resistance to in-plane stretch. Mast/luff and boom/foot nodes are pinned.

The sail has built-in bagginess: its flat fabric cut has 3.39% extra width at mid-height relative to the nominal rig chord, tapering to zero excess at the foot and head. Wind fills this excess cloth on either side. Each quadrilateral has one shear diagonal, making two hinged triangles rather than a locked tetrahedron. In-plane links use nearly inextensible 10 MN/m numerical stiffness; weak bending links allow folding. Small strain remains from compliance and incomplete constraint convergence; it is shown below the forces.

This is a coarse, quasi-steady attached-flow model. Outside forward ±20°, force numbers and vectors, pressure colors, smoke, and cloth updates are withheld. The gray sail shows the last simulated shape; returning to the supported range resumes without reset. Unsupported angle is distinct from a numerical failure. Stall, separation, turbulence, reversed-flow cloth behavior, wake roll-up, rig interference, ground effect, and cloth contact are not solved.

Each node’s mass is its tributary rest area times the cloth density. Gravity produces sag at zero/light wind; inertia and numerical damping govern how it relaxes. Fluid loads update at 60 Hz; cloth uses 80 constraint sweeps at 240 Hz and strong numerical damping (100/s). The flow assumes a stationary sail at each update, omitting cloth-motion boundary velocity and fluid added mass. Transient flutter and relaxation times are not physical predictions. Bending uses weak two-hop distance links, not a calibrated fabric shell.

The displayed residual tests only the control points; low residual does not establish real-world accuracy or impermeability between them. Runs exceeding 8% fabric strain stop visibly instead of clipping forces. Simulation may fall behind wall time on slow devices.
