COMPUTATIONAL SAIL LAB · MODEL NOTES

What the simulation calculates

A baggy, nearly inextensible fabric sail loads a three-dimensional vortex field. The field loads the cloth in return. This page explains the equations actually used in the demo.

Geometry & fullnessCloth & weightAirflowForcesSmokeNumerics & checksLimits

This is an attached-flow approximation. It calculates finite-span head/tip flow. It does not calculate viscous separation, stall, turbulence, or real broadside spilling. Smoke is integrated through the solved field, but that field has these limits.

1. Geometry, attachments, and built-in fullness

Use meters and seconds. The x axis follows the fixed boom, y points upward, and z is transverse to the flat rig plane. The mast is fixed into the ground at x = z = 0. The sail foot lies at y = 0.6 m, and its head at y = 5.0 m. Its chord tapers from 3.2 m to 0.25 m; the finite head chord prevents a degenerate top row.

Let u and v run from 0 to 1 across chord and height. The manufactured cloth reference is:

c(v)=3.2−2.95vccut(v)=c(v)[1+0.0339sin2(πv)]x→0(u,v)=(ccutu,0.6+4.4v,0)

This flat cut has 3.39% excess width at mid-height relative to the nominal rig chord, with zero excess at foot and head. It stores material fullness without a preferred side or prescribed curvature. Its metric is developable: a flat reference can fold into a bag on either side. The nominal upper rig chord is an outline for comparison, not an additional attachment; the free leech can move in all three coordinates.

ℓij0=‖x→0,i−x→0,j‖

Structural edges, one shear diagonal per quadrilateral, and weak two-hop bending proxies get their rest distances from this reference. Each quadrilateral is two triangles with a hinge, not a rigid tetrahedron. Wind may inflate, flatten, fold, or invert the available fullness; free-node rest coordinates are not imposed during simulation.

The earlier curved reference and both shear diagonals created an artificial shell-like inversion barrier. Its aerodynamic force changed sign on reversal, but the bag partly stayed on its original side. The flat cut and hinged triangulation fix the geometry of that membrane model. No wind-sign rule moves vertices, switches rest lengths, or resets the sail.

All luff nodes (u = 0) and all foot nodes (v = 0) are pinned exactly. Their positions never change, representing attachment to the fixed mast and boom. The leech and head have no positional pin except where they meet the luff. The initial free leech follows the wider flat-cut outline and can move afterward. The model omits rig collision and self-contact.

2. Cloth mass, gravity, and low stretch

The mesh has 77 nodes, with 120 hinged cloth triangles grouped into 60 aerodynamic quadrilaterals. Mass is lumped uniformly over each quadrilateral’s four nodes: each node receives one quarter of the rest area of each adjacent panel, including nodes on pinned boundaries:

mi=σcloth∑p∋iAp04,σcloth=0.22 kg/m²F→igravity=mig→,g→=(0,−9.81,0) m/s²

Total cloth mass is approximately 1.698 kg, computed from the flat cut’s rest area. Rest mass stays constant as the sail changes shape. For free nodes, inverse mass wᵢ = 1/mᵢ; pinned nodes use wᵢ = 0. Weight affects equilibrium sag. Inertia and numerical damping affect how the solver approaches that shape.

A distance constraint measures extension relative to the manufactured material length. XPBD gives it finite compliance α = 1/k:

Cij=‖x→i−x→j‖−ℓij0,α~=1kh2Δλ=−C−α~λwi+wj+α~Δx→i=wiΔλd^,Δx→j=−wjΔλd^

Here h = 1/240 s, λ is the accumulated multiplier within one cloth substep, and d̂ points from node j to i. Each substep resets λ to zero and applies 80 Gauss–Seidel sweeps. Structural and shear links use k = 10⁷ N/m, approximating near-inextensibility. Weak two-hop links use 0.5 N/m as a bending proxy; they are not calibrated shell bending or dihedral constraints. Finite compliance and incomplete convergence leave some extension, displayed as maximum link strain.

The actual prediction step applies force and gravity, then strong damping:

v→~=e−βh[v→+h(F→aerom+g→)],β=100 s⁻¹x→~=x→+hv→~

After constraints, velocity is recomputed from the corrected position difference divided by h. The strong damping aids equilibrium relaxation. It is not a physical prediction of settling time or flutter. The no-wind check measures gravitational sag against a gravity-free control while retaining exact rig anchors. With a perfectly flat start and no transverse force, gravity produces in-plane sag; it does not arbitrarily pick a side of the rig. In this stiff flat state the measured mass-center drop is only about 0.124 µm: the distance links largely carry weight in-plane, and spontaneous out-of-plane gravitational buckling is not resolved. This check confirms the weight term, not realistic draping.

3. The three-dimensional airflow field

The underlying approximation is incompressible, inviscid, attached potential flow away from a discrete vortex sheet. It is not a viscous Navier–Stokes calculation. Incoming velocity has strictly zero vertical component:

U→=U(cosθ,0,sinθ)V→(x→)=U→+∑jΓjb→j(x→)

U is speed in m/s, θ is direction, Γⱼ is circulation in m²/s, and bⱼ is the velocity per unit circulation of a horseshoe vortex. Each of 60 panels has a bound segment at quarter chord and a collocation point at three-quarter chord. Its two wake legs extend 70 m along incoming wind.

The unit-strength field of one finite segment from a to b uses the regularized Biot–Savart expression below. Define r₁ = x − a, r₂ = x − b, and d = b − a:

b→segment=r→1×r→24π(‖r→1×r→2‖2+ε2‖d→‖2)[d→·(r→1‖r→1‖−r→2‖r→2‖)]

ε = 0.035 m is a numerical vortex core. It smooths singular velocities rather than calculating viscous vortex diffusion. A horseshoe is the oriented sum of its bound segment and two downstream legs. Near an endpoint the implementation returns zero to avoid division by zero. Wake far ends are an approximation to infinity; no far-end closure segment is included.

No normal flow is enforced at each frozen-shape control point xᵢ with unit normal nᵢ. This gives a dense linear system solved with Gaussian elimination and partial pivoting:

∑jAijΓj=−U→·n→iAij=b→j(x→i)·n→i

The conventional quarter-chord/three-quarter-chord horseshoe arrangement and downstream legs provide an implicit Kutta closure. There is no independent resolved trailing-edge pressure test. Finite-span vortices generate vertical and transverse flow near the head and foot; above-head smoke therefore moves vertically despite horizontal incoming wind. The wake is straight and recomputed from the current shape, with no roll-up or shed wake history.

For reverse wind, chord ordering reverses so wake legs remain downstream; the free leech becomes the aerodynamic leading edge. This reversed regime is unsupported and withheld interactively. In raw diagnostic solves, the leading-edge switch changes bound and control locations and produces discontinuous, unreliable broadside loads, not a separated-flow prediction. Neither the mast nor boom nor ground modifies the calculated field.

4. Loads, pressure proxy, lift, and drag

Each bound segment has directed span vector Δl. Its local Kutta–Joukowski force uses incoming flow plus velocity from wake legs only at the segment midpoint. All bound-segment induced velocities are omitted from this force evaluation:

F→pfluid=ρairΓp(U→+V→wake)×Δl→F→pskin=12ρairU2ApCfe^D

ρair = 1.225 kg/m³, and êD is the incoming wind unit vector. The skin contribution uses an explicitly empirical Cf = 0.008 and the current panel area. It is not a resolved viscous calculation. Each panel’s combined model force is shared equally among its four corner nodes; forces assigned to pinned corners are supported by the fixed rig.

Panel color encodes a normal-load pressure proxy, pₚ = Fₚfluid · nₚ / Aₚ, in pascals. It is not a separately solved upper/lower surface pressure distribution. Blue means positive normal load, orange negative, on both faces. The normal follows material ordering (luff to leech crossed with foot to head), giving +z for a flat sail regardless of aerodynamic ordering. Gray means no supported fluid result.

Drag lies along incoming flow; lift is the entire perpendicular force, including its vertical part. The UI also gives signed horizontal lift along (−sin θ, 0, cos θ), and vertical force. Calculated induced drag and empirical skin drag appear separately. There is no separated-flow/profile drag model, so the displayed estimate is not accurate total drag of a real sail. Lift and drag arrows share 0.018 m/N scale; optional local arrows use 0.03 m/N.

5. Smoke follows the solved field

Thirty-five passive particles start at seven heights and five lateral offsets. Three heights, 4.8, 5.2, and 5.6 m, bracket the sail head. Their path histories are drawn as thin smoke trails. There are no prescribed curved paths or random drift.

dq→dt=V→(q→)q→n+1=q→n+ΔtV→(q→n+Δt2V→(q→n))

This is midpoint/RK2 integration, normally every 1/60 s of accumulated simulation time. The field is held fixed within each particle update. Trails are pathlines through changing fields, not instantaneous streamlines or continuously injected streaklines. They reset after long travel and retain at most 420 points. Smoke has no buoyancy, diffusion, volume, or effect on flow.

The algebraic boundary solve only constrains discrete control points. It does not guarantee zero penetration between them, so some tracers can pass through the coarse sail surface. Near-core paths cannot be interpreted as experimentally accurate flow.

6. Update order, defaults, and validation

  1. Every fourth cloth substep, build the vortex lattice from the current fabric shape.
  2. Solve circulation for frozen-shape no-penetration, then calculate and distribute aerodynamic panel forces.
  3. Predict cloth motion from held loads, gravity, and damping; project distance constraints; restore exact pins and recompute velocities.
  4. Integrate smoke through the latest field and render the WebGL scene. Pausing holds cloth and smoke while keeping the camera available.

This is partitioned, quasi-steady fluid–structure coupling. The fluid assumes a stationary shape at every solve: cloth-motion boundary velocity, added mass, and time-dependent wake shedding are omitted. Strong damping is numerical regularization. Neither relaxation time nor transient flutter is a physical prediction.

What was checked

The reproducible numerical checks test analytic Biot–Savart velocity, linear pivoting, zero wind, horizontal inflow, flat-sail lift symmetry and U² scaling, control-point boundary residuals, vector-force reconstruction, conservative force distribution, wake geometry, above-head vertical flow, tracer integration refinement, lattice refinement, low-stretch redistribution of manufactured fullness, gravity versus a weightless control, fixed anchors, bounded six-second runs through 12 m/s at ±20°, and visible numerical-stop/reset behavior.

The flat-lattice results change by less than 5% over these checks. The aerodynamic checks use the nominal rig outline; the interactive sail uses the wider flat cut. Default loaded cloth has about 0.9% residual link strain. The reversal regression fills +15°, −15°, +15°, and −15° for six simulated seconds each without resetting, verifies all free nodes settle on the loaded side, keeps rest geometry unchanged, and checks anchors, finite loads, and transient strain below 3%. Boundary residuals are near 10⁻¹⁵ m/s at control points. Above the flat-test head at (0.7, 5.2, 0.25) m, calculated vertical velocity is about −0.476 m/s. These checks establish internal consistency, not experimental accuracy.

The UI reports maximum link strain and boundary residual. Excessive circulation or a degenerate linear system withholds loads. A step exceeding 8% structural/shear strain is rolled back, and the run stops with a visible reason. The solver does not silently clip forces. Catch-up work is capped, so slow devices run slower than wall time rather than taking oversized timesteps.

7. How to interpret the result

Use modest forward incidence, roughly ±20°, for qualitative comparisons of shape, circulation, load direction, and finite-span head flow. Global incidence alone cannot establish attached flow on every curved panel. Outside forward ±20° (except zero wind), forces, load colors/vectors, smoke, and cloth updates are withheld. Gray cloth holds its last shape; returning within range resumes without reset. Unsupported physics is distinct from numerical failure.

The full requested separated-flow wind tunnel is not achieved by this model. It cannot determine stall onset, viscous separation, turbulence, rolled-up wakes, or real separated top spill. A viscous separated-flow solver with appropriate resolution and validation would be needed for those questions. The empirical skin coefficient is not a replacement for that solver.

The rigid rig has no flow obstruction; ground effects and fabric collisions are absent. Bending is a distance proxy, fabric properties are not calibrated, and incomplete constraint convergence causes small numerical extension. The flow core and panel spacing restrict detail. Low residual and numerical stability do not validate real sail efficiency.
